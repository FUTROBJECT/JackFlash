# JackFlash — Community Copy (canonical versions)

**Date:** 2026-08-22 · Companions: `docs/marketing-launch-plan.md` (§4 strategy), `JackFlash-AppStore-Listing.md` (store copy).

Rules of use (from the launch plan): written once, lightly adapted per venue, never blasted verbatim
to multiple communities. Lead with the story and the free tier, not the sell. Follow each community's
self-promo rules — some require mod approval or a dedicated thread; check before posting.

---

## 1. TestFlight beta ask (Phase A — post in 2–3 communities)

The 3-sentence honest ask. Adapt the first clause to the venue ("Fellow Primary Maths users —",
"Hi r/homeschool —", etc.).

> I built a Singapore Math practice app for my son Jack — real CPA method (concrete → pictorial →
> abstract), bar models, and mastery that means fast *and* unaided, not three lucky taps. Before it
> goes on the App Store I'd love critical feedback from parents who actually teach this way: TestFlight
> beta is free, the app has no ads, no accounts, and works fully offline. If you homeschool with
> Primary Mathematics, Dimensions, or Math in Focus, you're exactly who I want to hear from — link in
> [comments / per venue rules].

**Practical notes:**
- TestFlight public link: create in App Store Connect → TestFlight → Public Link after the first build uploads. Cap it (20–30) so the group stays conversational.
- Ask beta parents for: confusing moments, whether scaffolds feel like teaching or crutches, and their kid's reaction to the concrete (touch-the-math) mode.
- Keep a simple list of who joined from where — these are the launch-day promo-code recipients.

## 2. Founder story post (Phase B — canonical ~200 words)

The one honest post, adapted per venue. Title suggestions:
- r/homeschool: "I built a Singapore Math practice app for my son — Multiply & Divide is free, forever"
- Well-Trained Mind: "A CPA-faithful math facts app (built by a homeschool dad)"

> My son Jack is working through Singapore Math, and every practice app I tried had the same problems:
> subscriptions ($60–100/yr for flash cards), ads or tracking in apps aimed at eight-year-olds, and
> "mastery" that meant answering three questions with hints on.
>
> So I built the app I wanted. JackFlash does practice the way the CPA method actually teaches:
> concrete first (kids build the array with their hands), then pictorial (bar models and groups),
> then abstract (just the numbers). Scaffolds fade as understanding grows. A fact only counts as
> mastered when Jack answers it fast *and* unaided — the same standard his curriculum uses.
>
> The parts I refused to compromise on: no ads, no tracking, no accounts. Everything stays on the
> device — it works in airplane mode. Purchases sit behind a parent gate.
>
> Multiply & Divide is completely free — that's the whole times-tables journey, no catch. Fractions
> is a one-time $3.99 (first group free to try). No subscription, ever. More modules are coming as
> Jack reaches them.
>
> It's on the App Store now [link]. I'd genuinely value feedback from parents teaching this way —
> it shapes what I build next.

**Adaptation notes per venue:**
- r/homeschool / FB groups: keep as-is; put the link where rules allow (some require comments).
- Well-Trained Mind: slightly more curriculum-technical — name the CPA stages and bar models earlier.
- r/matheducation: reframe from parent story to pedagogy ("fluency-gated mastery, scaffold fading") — teachers, not parents.

## 3. Launch-day promo code note (to beta parents, individually)

> JackFlash is live! Here's a promo code for Fractions as a thank-you for the beta feedback: [CODE].
> Redeem: App Store → your profile picture → "Redeem Gift Card or Code." No obligation at all — but
> if the app's been useful, an App Store review genuinely helps a solo developer get found.

Compliance line (Apple): reviews may be *requested*, never incentivized or conditioned — the code is a
thank-you for beta feedback already given, stated before any mention of reviews, and the review ask is
explicitly no-obligation. Never "leave a review and I'll send a code."
