Questions

The things parents ask.

✓ Who is it for?

Grade 3 — Primary 3 in Singapore — so roughly ages 7 to 9, following the think! Mathematics sequence. Multiply & Divide covers the 2s through 10s; Fractions follows the P3 syllabus. Each child's profile is set to their own module, so a younger or older sibling can use it too.

✓ Is this really Singapore Math?

Yes — the CPA progression (concrete → pictorial → abstract), bar models, and number bonds, with scaffolds that fade as your child gets stronger. It's the same method as the books, not a product of the publisher.

✓ What does “mastered” mean?

Three correct answers. Then it moves to long-term review — the next day, then 3, 7, 14 and 30 days out — so it stays learned. Miss it on a review and it quietly drops back into practice.

✓ What happens when my child gets one wrong?

Two things: the fact comes back sooner, and the visual scaffold appears so they can see the math rather than guess. Getting it wrong is how the app finds out what to teach.

✓ Is Smart Practice AI?

No. It's rule-based: five categories and spaced repetition on a widening schedule — next day, then 3, 7, 14, 30. Every fact's category is visible in the app.

✓ Can I see what my child is struggling with?

Yes. The Parent Zone lists the exact facts with the lowest mastery, including ones they haven't tried yet.

✓ What data do you collect?

None. Nothing your child does leaves the device — no account, no analytics, no ads, and the app works with Wi-Fi off. The only exception is the purchase itself, which Apple or Google handle; we never see it.

✓ How many kids can use it?

Multiple kids share one device, each with their own profile, module and progress, behind a parent gate. A third-grader and her kindergarten sister can share one iPad.

✓ What's free?

Multiply & Divide is free forever. Fractions is $3.99, once, with Foundations free to try. Updates to a module you've bought are always included; new modules are $3.99 each, once.
